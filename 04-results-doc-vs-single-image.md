# 04 · Field note: document ads vs single image ads

**Real results from a B2B lead generation campaign. Client anonymised. All figures in AUD.**

---

## The results

| Format | Leads | CPL | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Document ads** (gated lead magnet) | 9 | $166 | Volume of top-of-funnel leads |
| **Single image ad** | 1 | $178 | **Converted into a paying client** |

On CPL alone, document ads look like the winner: more leads at a lower cost each.

On business outcome, the single image ad won: **one lead, one client, at $178 to acquire a customer.**

---

## What I learned

**1. CPL is the wrong scoreboard on its own.**
Document ads delivered 9× the leads at a lower CPL. The single image ad's lead cost more, but it turned into revenue. This is exactly why I report cost per qualified lead and cost per client, not CPL alone (see the [lead-quality ladder](https://github.com/vidhyaganapathy/growth-performance-playbook/blob/main/03-lead-quality-ladder.md)).

**2. Formats do different jobs.**
- **Document ads** attract people who want to *learn*: great for building an audience of interested prospects, and for retargeting.
- **Single image ads** with a direct offer attract people who are *ready to talk*: fewer leads, higher intent.

**3. The two work best together.**
Document ads warm up the market. Single image ads with a clear offer catch the people who are ready. Judging either in isolation misses how they feed each other.

---

## What I'd do next

- Retarget document-ad leads and openers with a single image offer ad
- Track every lead through to meeting and client, so each format is judged on cost per client
- Test a second single image offer to see whether the result repeats, since one client is a strong signal but a small sample

---

*Small sample sizes: treat these as directional, not benchmarks.*
