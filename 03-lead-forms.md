# 03 · Lead gen forms

LinkedIn lead gen forms pre-fill from the member's profile, so conversion rates are high. The risk is **easy leads that never become meetings.**

## Form setup

| Element | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| **Pre-filled fields** | Name, work email, company, job title |
| **Qualifying question** | One custom question that filters intent, e.g. "What's your timeline?" or "Team size?" |
| **Offer headline** | State exactly what they get |
| **Privacy policy** | Required; link to your policy |
| **Thank-you screen** | Deliver the asset and offer the next step (book a call, visit site) |

## Quality vs volume

| Lever | More volume | More quality |
|---|---|---|
| Number of fields | Fewer | One or two more, including a qualifying question |
| Offer | Broad guide | Specific tool, audit or assessment |
| Audience | Broader roles | Tighter ICP and matched audiences |

Pick based on what sales can handle and what converts to meetings.

## After the form

- [ ] Leads sync to the CRM or a sheet automatically (no manual exports)
- [ ] Follow-up goes out quickly, while intent is high
- [ ] Every lead is tagged with campaign, format and audience
- [ ] Sales marks leads as qualified or not, so you can report **cost per qualified lead** by format

## Metrics I report

| Metric | Why |
|---|---|
| Form open rate | Is the ad and offer compelling enough to start? |
| Form completion rate | Is the form too long? |
| CPL | Cost of volume |
| Cost per qualified lead | Cost of the leads that matter |
| Cost per meeting | Closest to pipeline |
